Machine learning & AI internship

Teaching a model
to watch climbing

Crux AI

May 2025 – August 2025 · College Station, TX

A climber's phone video already contains everything about the attempt — which holds, in what order, how the body moved between them. None of it is structured. Coaches were reviewing footage by eye: slow, inconsistent, and impossible to compare across sessions. I built the pipeline that makes it structured, then a language layer that makes it advice.

The pipeline

1 · Video inRaw climbing footage, ingested and walked frame by frame with OpenCV.
→
2 · Two modelsYOLOv11 hold detection and pose estimation run in parallel on every frame.
→
3 · SyncHold boxes matched against the 17-keypoint skeleton to map the body across the wall.
→
4 · StructurePer-attempt JSON: hold sequence, movement distance, joint trajectories, timing.
→
5 · WordsAn LLM layer turns those numbers into coaching notes a human can act on.

Precision labelling

Curated and annotated 7,000+ domain-specific images in Roboflow across 5 hold classes — jugs, crimps, slopers, pockets, volumes — with deliberate attention to edge cases and an augmentation strategy for the lighting chaos of a real gym.

Pose synchronisation

Frame-level sync of the 17-keypoint pose skeleton with hold detections, which is what makes grip-to-joint distance analytics and movement-path visualisation possible at all.

Shipping it

Dockerized inference services deployed on Microsoft Azure Container Apps, with MLOps practices so the models serve efficiently instead of living in a notebook.

The language layer

Structured metrics feed a prompt-engineered generative-AI pipeline producing strategy-level feedback on efficiency, route optimisation and technique — wrapped into athlete reports for coaches.

Delivered
